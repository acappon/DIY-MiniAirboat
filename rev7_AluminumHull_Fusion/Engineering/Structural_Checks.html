1. Steering pushrod: redesigned (31 Oct 2026). The old rod had a rod end at each end but jogged 14.2 in off the line between them. It would have yielded at about 33 lb of rod force, against the 160 lb that hard rudder at speed produces. Now every link is straight and pinned at both ends:

  • Horns: they point 3.5 in aft and 2 in outboard.
  • Tie bar (92 lb) and drive link (183 lb): both run behind the aft screen, under the rudders’ bottom edges.
  • Bellcrank: 3.5 / 4 in arms, on a 3/8 in steel bracket welded to the aft hoop at the lower rail.
  • Long rod: a single 100 in 1 × .065 rod (160 lb, buckling factor 4.4) runs outside the cage to the lever, which moved 1.5 in outboard.
Lever travel stays about ±10° for ±33° of rudder, and full rudder at speed takes about 61 lb at the handle. The steering reaction now lands at the hoop/rail corner instead of mid-rail.

Fixed in the rev 7 CAD, BOM and build guide (31 Oct 2026):

  1. Grip. The 1 in 316 stick was shown welded into the 6061 inner rail. That was my rev 7 error: stainless can’t be welded to aluminium. It is now a 1-1/8 in stick welded to a 3 × 2 × 1/4 in 316 base plate. Four 1/4 in 316 bolts clamp the plate through the console top and both rail walls, with crush tubes, Tef-Gel and a gasket. Stick factor 1.31 → 2.24; bolts 3.56.
  2. Prop cage. The side bays and the drum had no diagonals, so the tubes carried the loads in bending. Now:
    • DOM tube (yield 50 ksi or better) throughout.
    • Hoops 1 × .083 (were .049; .065 was enough for the G13, .083 is needed for the stronger G13B slipstream).
    • Longitudinals and aft screen bars 1 × .065 DOM (were 3/4 and 1/2 EMT).
    • A 1 × .065 diagonal in each side bay, from the aft foot up to the forward leg 3 in below the hoop.
    Lowest factor 0.71 → 1.87. Kickers from the rudder rails were tried and dropped: they would cross the prop disk.
  3. Trim-tab bracket. A 3/8 × 2 in 6061 flat bar is riveted inside the transom behind it, from the bottom frame angle to the rub rail (0.41 → 2.47). A 1.5 in angle would have hit the aft floor panel, which starts 0.55 in inside the transom.
  4. Driver side frame. Posts, arms, inner rail, knee braces and diagonal are now 3/16 in wall (1.28 → 1.66, skins not counted). This adds 5.6 lb.
  5. Prop shaft. Now specified as 4140 pre-hardened, 28–32 HRC (fatigue factor 1.56 → 2.15), with the thrust carried on a shoulder or clamped collar.
  6. Forward A-frame crossbar. Now 2 × 3 × 1/8, 3 in deep (static factor 1.68 → 5.46). Its belt-ripple stress range drops from 4.1 to 0.9 ksi. The build guide now says to check the tie-strut bolts are tight before every run: without the struts each aft engine mount would see about 1018 lb of uplift.

Together these add about 22 lb (empty boat 1,247 lb by the CAD weight model). Every changed part was interference-checked: no new clashes.

2 · Method and criteria

  • Frame models: 3D linear frames (6 degrees of freedom per node, Euler-Bernoulli beams), solved in calc/frame3d.py, which reproduces the textbook cantilever exactly. Drive tower: four A-frame legs, two crossbars, pinned clevis braces, and a rigid head standing for the peak plates and yoke. Each case is run with fixed and with pinned feet, and the worse result is kept. Cage: both hoops in 36+ chords, 8 longitudinals, face bars, rudder rails, legs on fixed pads. Side frame: posts on the base plate and hull-side clips, top rail, cross arms, bowed braces in 8 chords, inner rail, diagonal.
  • Stresses: axial plus bending at each element end; square tubes add both bending axes; torsion is combined by von Mises.
  • Allowables:
    • Welded 6061-T6 members are checked against the heat-affected yield of 15 ksi (ADM) everywhere, because the peak moments sit at the welds.
    • Cage steel: A513 Type 1 minimum, 32 ksi, as drawn; DOM at 50 ksi or better with the fixes. EMT: 30 ksi.
    • Bolts: stainless F593 condition CW, 100 ksi; grade 8, 150 ksi.
    • Aluminium bearing in the heat-affected zone: 2 × 24 ksi × d × t.
  • Required safety factors: 1.65 on yield and buckling for operating loads (ADM / AISC allowable-stress level); 1.0 for misuse loads (no permanent bend); 2.0 on bolts and pins; 2.0 on shaft fatigue (Goodman).
  • Fatigue screen: the stress range from ±25% belt-load ripple, compared with 2 ksi. That is roughly the constant-amplitude limit of common welded aluminium details (ADM Chapter J, categories D–E). The reviewer should confirm the detail category.

8c · Bottom plating to ISO 12215-5

Bottom plating: now 0.125 in, with frames added at 60, 84 and 111.5 in (31 Oct 2026). The original 0.090 in bottom was thinner than ISO 12215-5 requires: its worst panels, between frames 24 in apart, needed 0.131–0.176 in. As built, the boat passes as a category D (sheltered waters) planing cruiser with the dynamic load factor limited to 3.0 g: the worst bay needs 0.117 in. In category C (inshore) it needs 0.126 in, within 1%. At the speed-based 5.4 g it would need up to 0.157 in, so the 3.0 g basis must be written into the owner’s manual: a planing cruiser, slow down in waves. The hat stringers pass with large margins. The 1/4 in UHMW is not counted.

Inputs:

  • Loaded displacement 838 kg (1847 lb): empty with the heavier bottom, plus the 508 lb capacity, plus full fuel.
  • Loaded waterline length 3.20 m, chine beam 1.83 m.
  • Deadrise taken as 10°: the bottom is flat, and the standard’s minimum is 10°.
  • Top speed 33.9 knots.
  • Plating design stress 129 MPa: 5052-H32 unwelded, the lesser of 0.6σu and 0.9σy. The bottom is riveted, with no welds.

Dynamic load factor nCG: its first equation gives 15.2 g, because the bottom is flat, the boat is light and it’s fast. Two lower values are shown:

  • 5.4 g, from the standard’s alternative speed-based equation.
  • 3.0 g, the limit for planing cruisers. Using it requires the owner’s manual to state that operation, for example: slow down in waves.

9 · Limits of this analysis

  • Load estimates: thrust, prop inertia, belt tension, rudder coefficients and tab lift are all estimates. A static thrust test and a weighed prop would firm up the two biggest.
  • Simplified joints and parts: joints are modelled as rigid welds or ideal pins, and the peak plates and yoke as one rigid body. The cage mesh and console skins are left out as stiffeners (conservative). The tower is modelled as rigid on the bed plates; the beds and stringers aren’t modelled.
  • Fatigue: screened, not calculated. Weld quality dominates real fatigue life of welded aluminium drive frames, so inspect the A-frame welds after the first 10 hours and every season.
  • Not covered: blade failure (no cage stops a thrown blade), trailer and tie-down loads, and hull bottom slamming.

10 · Reviewer checklist

  1. Confirm the design loads in section 1, especially thrust, the belt hub factor and the 3 g / 1 g accelerations.
  2. Confirm the HAZ allowables and the fatigue detail category for the A-frame crossbar and tab welds.
  3. Review the steering linkage, grip mounting, cage bracing and side-frame walls as built.
  4. Replace the 0.75 psi transom pressure with ISO 12215-5 values, and confirm the section 8c bottom-plating equations and the design category.
  5. Check the Savitsky flap lift used for the trim tab against the tab’s real maximum angle.
