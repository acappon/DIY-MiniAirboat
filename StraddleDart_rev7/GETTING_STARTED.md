# Getting started with the Straddle Dart

The Straddle Dart is a 12.5 ft open-source riveted-aluminium airboat for one or two people, powered by a Suzuki G13B car engine (1.3 L twin-cam, about 100 hp) through a belt redrive. It's designed to be built in a backyard shop by someone who can rivet, bend sheet, and TIG-weld aluminium.

## Operating limits

The hull is designed to ISO 12215-5 as a **design category D (sheltered waters) planing cruiser**: winds up to Beaufort force 4 and waves up to about 0.3 m (1 ft), with occasional waves to 0.5 m.

- **Slow down in waves and chop.** The bottom plating is sized for the slamming loads of a planing cruiser driven with care, not for jumping waves at full speed. A flat-bottomed boat this light and fast slams hard.
- **Stay in sheltered water:** the Everglades, canals, rivers and small lakes. Avoid open bays and the big lakes when the wind is up.
- **Load:** stay within the capacity label, 2 persons / 400 lb and 490 lb total.

See `Engineering/Structural_Checks.html`, section 8c, for the calculation.

## Why the cockpit looks like this

If you've seen airboats before, this one's cockpit will look odd. That's on purpose.

### The usual airboat

Most airboats carry an aircraft engine bolted directly to the prop, so the engine sits up at prop height. The driver usually sits several feet up too, often on what looks like a plastic patio chair, to see over the bow. Both put a lot of weight high up. The driver also has very little to hold on to: one hand on the steering stick, and feet on a flat deck.

Hit a wake or a log at speed in that seat and you get thrown around. You have to keep steering while just trying to stay aboard.

### Why not a seat belt?

Seat belts are a safety liability on a boat, and more so on an airboat.

- **Entrapment:** if the boat flips or swamps, a belted driver can be trapped under it or held in the water. An airboat that rolls over usually floats upside down, with the engine, prop and cage hanging underneath like a keel. You can't right it by hand, and you don't want to be strapped to it.
- **The machinery:** a belted driver can't get away from the engine, exhaust, prop and cage, which are hot, heavy and sharp when things go wrong.
- **Getting clear:** in a capsize, being thrown clear is far safer than being held in.

The right tether on this boat is the **kill-switch lanyard** on the dash, clipped to you. If you leave the seat, the engine stops. Wear a **life jacket**; there's a hook for one inside the console's outboard panel.

### How this cockpit holds you instead

This seating is meant to give the driver a firm connection to the boat, safely, without a seat belt. You're held by your own hands, feet, knees and thighs, so you can let go in an instant.

**The handle.** The fixed Hurst T-handle by your right hand does three jobs:
1. **A strong point to hang on to.** From there you can pull yourself back against the seat back. It's rated for a 450 lb pull in any direction. The handle is a real Hurst shift knob, locked and pinned to a stud on a 1-1/8 in stainless stick; the stick and its plate, bolted through the console top and frame, are built for 600 lb.
2. **Feedback.** It's bolted through the frame to the hull side, so you feel through your hand what the hull is doing.
3. **Body English.** It lets you throw your weight one way or the other to change how the hull rides and turns.

**The foot ladders.** Two short ladders, one per foot, rise toward the bow. Their rungs work like motorcycle footpegs: push on them and you press yourself back into the seat back. The throttle pedal clamps to any rung of the right-hand ladder, so you can set it to suit your leg length.

**The knee braces.** The padded bars to starboard, under the console, are curved to fit a big leg. If you drive aggressively, clamp the throttle on a rung that lets you brace your right knee against them. Then your hand, foot, knee and back all hold you in place.

**Sitting astride the bench.** You sit astride the storage compartment, so you can squeeze it between your thighs, like gripping a horse or a motorcycle tank.

**Getting in and out.** The armrest swings up and back out of the way, then drops back down as you settle in.

### What it does for stability

All of this keeps the driver low. The seat cushion is only 18.5 in above the hull bottom.

The engine is low too. It drives the prop through a belt, so the engine's crankshaft sits 15 in above the hull bottom while the prop hub is at 52.5 in. On a typical airboat, the engine sits at the prop's height.

With both the driver and the engine low, this boat should be harder to flip than most airboats. It isn't flip-proof, and nothing is: still avoid hard turns at speed, and read the swamped-stability warning in the engineering report.

### Or just relax

None of this is mandatory. On a calm run you can sit back comfortably, with your leg stretched out to a more forward rung and a hand just resting on the handle in case something unexpected comes along.

And there's a handy cup holder just forward of the grip, because this is America.
